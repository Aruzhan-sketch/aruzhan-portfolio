# Lessons from the Jessup Moot Court Competition

*Posted: September 2026*

## Introduction

In February 2026, I had the extraordinary opportunity to participate in the Phillip C. Jessup International Law Moot Court Competition—first at the national rounds in Tashkent, Uzbekistan, and then at the international rounds in Washington D.C. These experiences were transformative, challenging my thinking about advocacy, research, and international law. I was honored to receive the Best Oralist Award at the national rounds.

In this post, I'd like to share the lessons I learned and reflections on what made this competition such a valuable learning experience.

## What is the Jessup Moot Court?

For those unfamiliar, the Jessup is the world's largest international law moot court competition. Teams of law students from universities around the globe compete in simulated international court proceedings, arguing hypothetical cases before "judges" (usually experienced lawyers and academics).

### Why It Matters
- It brings together young legal minds from diverse backgrounds
- It demonstrates law students' skills in research, writing, and oral advocacy
- It fosters international dialogue and cultural exchange
- It contributes to the development of international law itself

## The National Rounds: Tashkent Experience

### Preparation
The months before competition were intense. Our team:
- Researched complex questions of international law
- Drafted written memorials (legal briefs) arguing both sides
- Practiced oral advocacy until we could argue effectively on our feet
- Refined our arguments through countless practice rounds

### Key Lessons from Nationals

**Lesson 1: Preparation is everything (but so is flexibility)**

We prepared extensively, but during oral arguments, judges asked questions we hadn't anticipated. We had to think on our feet, apply our legal knowledge creatively, and respond thoughtfully rather than reciting prepared remarks.

*Takeaway*: Deep knowledge allows flexibility. You can't memorize your way to good advocacy—you need to truly understand your position.

**Lesson 2: Listen more than you talk**

The best advocates I observed weren't the ones who delivered long speeches. They were the ones who carefully listened to judges' questions, addressed their concerns directly, and engaged in genuine dialogue.

*Takeaway*: Listening is a skill. In advocacy (and life), understanding what others are thinking is more important than waiting for your turn to speak.

**Lesson 3: Humility strengthens your arguments**

The strongest advocates acknowledged when a question was difficult, admitted when opposing counsel made a good point, and explained respectfully why they still believed their position was right. This honesty made judges trust them more.

*Takeaway*: Admitting what you don't know, and what's difficult about your position, actually makes you more credible than pretending everything is simple.

## The International Rounds: Washington D.C.

Advancing to the international rounds was a dream come true. Competing in Washington D.C. against teams from universities around the world elevated the experience to another level entirely.

### The Washington Experience

- **Scale**: Teams from over 100 countries competing
- **Intensity**: Arguments before very accomplished judges
- **Diversity**: Interacting with legal minds from different traditions and backgrounds
- **Prestige**: Competing in the capital of a major global power

### Additional Lessons from Internationals

**Lesson 4: Diverse perspectives strengthen legal thinking**

At the international rounds, I encountered legal arguments I'd never considered. Advocates from different countries, with different legal traditions, approached problems in ways that challenged my assumptions.

*Takeaway*: If you only hear from people like you, you'll have blind spots. Seek out perspectives that challenge you.

**Lesson 5: International law is about building bridges**

International law at its best isn't about winning arguments—it's about finding common ground and building systems where all nations can coexist. The best advocates understood this deeper purpose.

*Takeaway*: In any field, the "win" that creates more problems isn't really a win. Look for solutions that work for everyone.

**Lesson 6: Your voice matters**

As someone from Kazakhstan, in the heartland of Central Asia, I was aware of participating in a largely Euro-American-dominated legal establishment. Yet I realized that *my* voice, *my* perspective from Central Asia, was valuable and often underrepresented.

*Takeaway*: Whether in law or any field, representation matters. Don't assume you don't belong at "prestigious" spaces.

## Oral Advocacy: The Skill Behind the Award

The Best Oralist Award was deeply meaningful to me. Receiving it taught me about effective oral advocacy and communication:

### What Makes Effective Oral Advocacy?

1. **Clarity**: Using simple, direct language to explain complex ideas
2. **Confidence**: Standing firmly in your position while remaining respectful
3. **Engagement**: Making eye contact, speaking at a natural pace, showing genuine passion
4. **Responsiveness**: Addressing judges' actual concerns, not delivering a speech
5. **Authenticity**: Being yourself rather than adopting a false "lawyer persona"
6. **Preparation**: Having mastered your material so thoroughly that you can discuss it naturally

### My Approach
- Practice obsessively until arguments become second nature
- Record myself and watch videos critically
- Seek feedback from mentors and peers
- Remember that judges are looking for the best legal reasoning, not performance
- Connect genuinely with the courtroom experience

## Beyond the Competition: Broader Reflections

### On Legal Education
This competition taught me that legal education should develop more than knowledge—it should develop judgment, wisdom, and the ability to see complex issues from multiple angles.

### On International Engagement
Competing internationally reinforced my belief in the value of cross-cultural dialogue. Despite different legal systems, languages, and traditions, we were united in trying to advance international law.

### On Personal Growth
This experience pushed me outside my comfort zone in ways that accelerated my growth. I developed confidence, deepened my legal knowledge, and built lasting connections with legal minds around the world.

## Lessons for Life Beyond Law

While I focused on international law through this competition, the lessons extend far beyond:

- **Master your field deeply** so you can navigate uncertainty
- **Listen to others** with genuine curiosity
- **Stay humble** about what you know and don't know
- **Seek diverse perspectives** and let them challenge you
- **Remember the bigger purpose** behind what you do
- **Show up authentically** in professional spaces
- **Never stop growing** and learning

## Looking Forward

The Jessup competition changed my perspective on international law and my place in the global legal community. It affirmed my passion for law, policy, and social change. Whether or not I continue in legal practice, these lessons about advocacy, dialogue, and international cooperation will guide my work.

## For Future Competitors

If you're considering participating in the Jessup or similar competitions, I'd encourage you to:

1. **Commit fully** - These competitions require significant time and energy, but the returns are immense
2. **Surround yourself with good people** - A strong team makes all the difference
3. **Embrace the challenge** - You'll be competing against some of the best legal minds in your generation
4. **Remember the bigger picture** - You're not just competing; you're contributing to international dialogue
5. **Enjoy the experience** - Make memories, build friendships, and savor the intellectual challenge
