# Digital Rights and Cybersecurity Awareness: Why Every Student Should Care

*Posted: September 2026*

## Introduction

On September 5-6, 2026, I participated in "Digital Dignity: Civil Society Against Cyberbullying," an awareness event organized by ValEUs and Nazarbayev University for high school students. The event aimed to raise awareness about cyberbullying and inform young people about their digital rights and responsibilities.

This experience reinforced my conviction that digital literacy and cybersecurity awareness are essential life skills for the generation growing up entirely within digital environments. In this post, I'd like to share why this matters and what young people (and adults!) should know about digital rights.

## The Digital Generation

Today's high school students are the first generation to grow up with smartphones, social media, and constant connectivity. While this offers incredible opportunities, it also presents unique challenges:

- **Digital presence from childhood** - Most have an online footprint that extends back years
- **Permanence of digital actions** - What they post can follow them indefinitely
- **Social pressure amplified** - Peer dynamics play out in public digital spaces
- **New forms of harm** - Cyberbullying, harassment, and digital exploitation are real threats

## Understanding Cyberbullying

### What is Cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is harassment or bullying conducted through digital platforms. Unlike traditional bullying, it has unique characteristics:

- **24/7 availability** - It doesn't stop when school ends
- **Audience amplification** - Harmful content can reach hundreds or thousands instantly
- **Permanence** - Screenshots and shares mean content can resurface years later
- **Anonymity** - Perpetrators often hide behind anonymous accounts, lowering inhibitions
- **Blurred accountability** - It's sometimes unclear who is responsible or how to respond

### Forms of Cyberbullying

- **Harassment** - Repeated insulting or threatening messages
- **Public humiliation** - Sharing embarrassing photos or information
- **Exclusion** - Being deliberately left out of group chats or online communities
- **Impersonation** - Creating fake accounts pretending to be someone else
- **Spreading rumors** - Sharing false information to damage reputation
- **Doxing** - Sharing personal information without consent

## Why Cyberbullying Harms

The effects of cyberbullying can be severe:

### Emotional & Psychological Impact
- Anxiety and depression
- Loss of self-esteem and confidence
- Social withdrawal
- Academic decline
- In severe cases, self-harm or suicidal ideation

### Academic & Social Effects
- Difficulty concentrating in school
- Avoidance of peers and social situations
- Loss of friendships
- School avoidance or dropout

### Long-term Consequences
- Trauma that persists into adulthood
- Difficulty forming relationships
- Lasting impact on digital behavior and trust

## Digital Rights: What Everyone Should Know

Beyond avoiding harm, it's crucial to understand your digital rights:

### Right to Privacy
- Your personal data and digital communications are yours
- You have the right to control who sees your information
- Companies cannot use your data without consent
- Review privacy settings on all platforms regularly

### Right to Safety
- You have the right to be safe online
- Harassment and threats are not acceptable
- Platforms should provide tools to block and report abusers
- In some cases, cybercrime is prosecutable by law

### Right to Be Forgotten
- In some jurisdictions, you can request removal of certain personal information
- You can delete your own posts and content
- Organizations must respond to data deletion requests
- (Note: Complete deletion may not always be possible due to screenshots/shares)

### Right to Digital Literacy Education
- Understanding digital platforms and risks should be taught in schools
- Everyone deserves to know how to protect themselves online
- Critical thinking about digital content is essential

### Right to Anonymity (with Responsibility)
- You can choose to use pseudonyms or remain anonymous
- However, anonymity doesn't eliminate responsibility for your actions
- Using anonymity to harm others is abuse of this right

## Practical Steps to Protect Yourself

### Before You Post

- **Think before sharing** - Will I be comfortable with this information being public permanently?
- **Adjust privacy settings** - Limit who can see your content
- **Avoid sharing personal details** - Be cautious with location, phone number, school name, etc.
- **Consider the context** - Is this something that could be misinterpreted or used against you?

### If You Experience Cyberbullying

1. **Don't retaliate** - Responding with hostility often escalates conflict
2. **Save evidence** - Screenshot messages and posts
3. **Block the person** - Most platforms allow blocking and reporting
4. **Tell a trusted adult** - Parents, teachers, or school counselors can help
5. **Report to the platform** - Most social media sites have reporting mechanisms
6. **Report to authorities** - In cases of serious threats or harassment, contact police

### Creating a Positive Digital Culture

- **Be kind online** - Your words have impact even through a screen
- **Defend others** - If you see someone being bullied, speak up or report it
- **Diversify your feeds** - Follow accounts that inspire and educate you
- **Take breaks** - Digital wellness matters; disconnect regularly
- **Model good behavior** - Be the example you want to see

## The Role of Platforms and Communities

### What Platforms Should Do
- Provide robust reporting and blocking tools
- Respond quickly to abuse reports
- Design features that reduce harm (e.g., think-before-you-post prompts)
- Educate users about safety
- Make accountability structures transparent

### What Communities Should Do
- Create safe, inclusive spaces online
- Hold members accountable for respectful behavior
- Support people who experience harassment
- Educate about digital rights and responsibilities
- Advocate for platform accountability

### What Parents Should Do
- Stay engaged with children's digital lives without being invasive
- Model good digital citizenship
- Teach critical thinking about online content
- Create open channels for discussing problems
- Set reasonable limits on screen time

### What Schools Should Do
- Integrate digital literacy into curriculum
- Address cyberbullying through education and consequences
- Create peer support systems
- Teach empathy and perspective-taking
- Partner with parents and communities

## Looking Forward: A Hopeful Vision

Digital technology is here to stay, and it offers incredible opportunities for connection, learning, and growth. The goal isn't to eliminate technology but to use it more thoughtfully and safely.

I believe in a digital future where:
- Young people feel empowered and safe online
- Technology enhances rather than diminishes human connection
- Diversity and different perspectives are celebrated
- Harm is quickly addressed through community accountability
- Digital rights are understood and protected globally

## My Takeaway

After facilitating discussions with high school students about digital dignity, I was reminded that this generation is more aware of digital issues than previous ones. They're thinking about these challenges, and many want to create positive online communities.

The work of protecting digital dignity belongs to all of us—platforms, educators, parents, policymakers, and young people themselves. By working together and maintaining focus on core values of respect, safety, and inclusion, we can create digital spaces that truly serve human flourishing.
